Roadmap

Three paths, four stages each: about eight months for IT, about nine for information systems and about ten for computer science at 5 to 8 hours a week. Finish each stop before moving to the next.

  • IT path
  • Computer science path
  • Information systems path

IT path

For support, networking, systems and cloud roles.

  1. Foundations

    Weeks 1 to 10

    Learn how computers and networks behave before you touch anything advanced.

    • IT support basics
    • Networking fundamentals

  2. Systems and scripting

    Weeks 11 to 20

    Get comfortable on the command line and start automating small jobs.

    • Linux and the command line
    • Python for IT automation
    • Databases and SQL

  3. Choose a focus

    Weeks 21 to 32

    Pick one or two areas that match the job you want. You do not need all of them.

    • Cybersecurity fundamentals
    • Cloud essentials
    • Web development basics
    • DevOps and automation

  4. Prove it

    Ongoing

    Turn what you know into something an employer can check.

    • Pass one entry-level certification (see the table below)
    • Publish your mini projects with short write-ups
    • Build a home lab with virtual machines

Certifications to consider

Certifications are optional, but they give hiring managers a common yardstick. Pick one that matches your focus.

CertificationBest afterWhat it covers
Google IT Support Professional CertificateFoundationsSupport, networking, operating systems, security basics
CompTIA A+FoundationsHardware, operating systems, troubleshooting
CompTIA Network+FoundationsNetworking concepts, infrastructure, troubleshooting
CompTIA Security+Choose a focusThreats, controls, risk and incident response
AWS Certified Cloud PractitionerChoose a focusAWS services, pricing and cloud concepts
Microsoft Azure Fundamentals (AZ-900)Choose a focusAzure services, security and governance

Exam objectives and prices change. Check the vendor's website before you book.

Computer science path

For software development, research and further study. Expect more math and more programming than the IT path.

  1. Programming and math

    Weeks 1 to 12

    Learn to write working programs and to reason about them precisely.

    • Programming fundamentals
    • Discrete math and logic

  2. Core computer science

    Weeks 13 to 28

    Understand how data is organized, how machines execute code and how systems share resources.

    • Data structures and algorithms
    • Computer architecture
    • Operating systems

  3. Go deeper

    Weeks 29 to 44

    Choose by interest. Theory suits research, engineering suits industry, and machine learning suits data work.

    • Theory of computation
    • Software engineering
    • AI and machine learning basics
    • Databases and SQL and networking as electives

  4. Prove it

    Ongoing

    Employers and admissions committees want evidence you can solve problems, not only pass courses.

    • Solve practice problems regularly, for example with Advent of Code
    • Publish your mini projects with clear READMEs
    • Contribute to an open-source project
    • Rehearse explaining your solutions out loud before technical interviews

Information systems path

For analyst, ERP, data and project roles that sit between business teams and technical teams. Expect more writing, modeling and people skills than the other paths.

  1. Business and tech basics

    Weeks 1 to 10

    Learn what a business needs from its systems and how the computers behind them work.

    • Business analysis basics
    • IT support basics

  2. Data and systems design

    Weeks 11 to 26

    Store business data properly and design systems that people can build and use.

    • Databases and SQL
    • Data modeling and database design
    • Systems analysis and design

  3. Choose a focus

    Weeks 27 to 38

    Pick two areas that match the role you want. You do not need all of them.

    • ERP and business processes
    • Data analytics and dashboards
    • IT project management and governance
    • Cybersecurity fundamentals

  4. Prove it

    Ongoing

    Show that you can turn a business problem into a working design.

    • Consider an entry-level credential such as IIBA ECBA, PMI CAPM or ITIL 4 Foundation (check the provider for current exam details)
    • Publish a portfolio with one requirements document, one data model and one dashboard
    • Practice explaining technical ideas to non-technical people
